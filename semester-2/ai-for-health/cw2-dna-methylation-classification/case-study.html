01 · Margins beat neighbours

SVC beat a KNN comparator on identical features. The boundary between subtypes — especially Group 3/4 — is smooth and non-linear, and needs a margin-based model rather than nearest-neighbour voting.

02 · Weighting mattered most

WNT is 51 of 1,370 samples. class_weight='balanced' — a 3.4× heavier penalty on WNT errors — lifted macro-F1 more than any kernel or hyperparameter change that followed.

03 · It held up on new hardware

Projected onto 97 samples run on a newer EPIC array, never seen in training or tuning, the model lost only 0.03 of macro-F1.

Methods

Margin-based, weighted for imbalance, tuned in five steps

SVC was chosen over KNN, decision trees, and logistic regression because the subtype boundaries in NMF space are smooth and non-linear rather than governed by simple proximity or trees' axis-aligned splits — and the approach is validated on this exact HM450/EPIC pipeline by Abid & Rafiee (2024, weighted F1 0.95, polynomial kernel). KNN served only as a check: had it matched SVC, the subtypes would have been trivially separable by proximity alone. It didn't.

All six HM450 errors were low-confidence — under 70% certainty, with the correct subtype as runner-up — and three of six confuse neighbouring SHH variants, consistent with known intragroup heterogeneity rather than model failure. The clearest caveat: the 16 NMF features were fit on all 1,370 samples before this train/test split, so test performance isn't fully independent of training data; a stricter pipeline would fit NMF on the training set alone. WNT also remains thin at around four samples per fold under 10-fold CV, so its share of a very clean topline number rests on a noisy estimate.

Data

Eight subtypes, badly imbalanced

1,370 medulloblastoma samples, each reduced from raw HM450 methylation beta-values to 16 NMF metagene features in Part 1 of this coursework. The eight consensus molecular subtypes are nowhere near evenly sized.
