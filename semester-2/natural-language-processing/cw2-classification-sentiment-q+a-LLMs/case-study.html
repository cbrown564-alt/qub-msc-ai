The bigger transformer memorised the training set — and still lost

Four encoder architectures — Word2Vec, BERT, ModernBERT, and RoBERTa/DistilBERT — were applied to the same 602-story corpus from Part 1: fine-tuned country classification, zero-shot sentiment and emotion analysis, and extractive question-answering. The sharpest result wasn't which model won — it's why the newer, larger one didn't: ModernBERT drove its training loss to 0.0009 and still generalised worse than BERT.

01 · Bigger model, worse generalisation

ModernBERT reached a training loss of 0.0009 — roughly 400× lower than BERT's 0.37 — yet its test accuracy still trailed BERT's 89% at 86%. On 602 stories, more parameters bought overfitting, not accuracy.

02 · Sentiment barely tells themes apart

Zero-shot RoBERTa sentiment was dominated by neutral (61–65%) across all five themes; only Scientific Discovery stood out as clearly more positive. A same-architecture classifier predicting theme from a story's last sentence scored a Cohen's kappa of just 0.24, against 0.78 for country.

03 · Fine-tuning fixes QA — except long answers

Fine-tuning BERT-SQuAD2 lifted token-F1 from 0.42 to 0.96 for Hard-Boiled stories and 0.44 to 0.91 for stories written for children. Descriptive stories barely moved, 0.29 to 0.41: F1 falls monotonically as answer length grows, fine-tuned or not.

Methods

Four architectures, staged from bag-of-words to fine-tuned encoders

Word2Vec with mean-pooled embeddings and logistic regression served as a non-contextual floor for country classification; BERT and ModernBERT then added an identical [CLS]-token classification head, isolating what a newer, larger, longer-context encoder buys on just 602 stories. ModernBERT needed heavy correction to stay usable — dropout raised from 0.0 to 0.5, learning rate to 3e-5, and early stopping with patience 3 (triggered at epoch 15) — and even then its learning curves stayed less stable than BERT's clean 3-epoch run. RoBERTa and DistilBERT were used zero-shot for sentiment and emotion, since no ground-truth labels exist to fine-tune against; BERT-SQuAD2 was instead fine-tuned directly, using Hugging Face's offset-mapping pipeline to align 648 character-level answer spans from three writing styles to sub-word token positions across sliding windows.

The fine-tuned QA numbers carry a stated caveat: they're measured on the same 648 examples used to train the model, so they show what domain fine-tuning can achieve here, not how well it generalises to unseen stories. The zero-shot QA baseline is the fairer generalisation reference, and it shows the same shape — mean token-F1 fell from 0.50 on answers under 20 characters to 0.25 on answers over 100, before any fine-tuning happened. Classification validation and test sets are thin (22 samples per label for the country task), so BERT's −2pp and ModernBERT's −9pp generalisation gaps, while consistent with the training-loss story, rest on a small test set. Sentiment and emotion scores carry model-specific baseline biases too — DistilBERT's anger runs high, RoBERTa's joy runs high — so only within-model, relative comparisons are treated as meaningful, never absolute scores across models.

Data

602 LLM-generated stories, reused whole from Part 1

The same corpus built in Part 1: 602 short stories, each generated from a template combining theme, setting, protagonist, writing style, country, and two random object concepts. A stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split is applied consistently across every task. Two of the corpus's four countries — China and Northern Ireland — are almost perfectly balanced at 144 stories each, which is why country, not the five-way theme label, was chosen as the classification target used to compare model architectures head-to-head.
