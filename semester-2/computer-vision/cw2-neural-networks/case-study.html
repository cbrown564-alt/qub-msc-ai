01 · Flattening pixels throws away locality

All three MLP configs (40.1–45.2%) lost to the hand-crafted baseline (55.9%), despite the widest config carrying 27.3M parameters — 3.5× more than the CNN that eventually won.

02 · Residual connections did what depth couldn't

CNN-VGG, trained with identical optimiser, schedule and augmentation, only matched the baseline (52.1%). Swapping its plain conv/max-pool stack for CNN-Res's strided downsampling and skip connections added +19.2pt for 2.3M more parameters.

03 · Augmentation was the difference between memorising and generalising

Trained without it, CNN-Res hit 99.5% train accuracy but only 64.1% test (a 35.4pt gap). With it, train accuracy fell to 84.0% and test rose to 71.3% (a 12.7pt gap).

Methods

Same optimiser, same schedule, six architectures — and one learning-rate sweet spot

The three MLP configs (shallow 512→256, deep 1024→512→256→128, wide 2048→1024) isolate depth versus width on flattened pixel input. CNN-VGG and CNN-Res isolate a single architectural change: both use the same Conv2d/BatchNorm/ReLU/Dropout2d building blocks, but CNN-VGG downsamples with max-pooling while CNN-Res replaces it with strided convolutions and residual blocks. Every model uses Adam (lr=1e-3, weight decay=1e-4), ReduceLROnPlateau (patience=5, factor=0.5) and early stopping — patience 20 for MLPs, 10 for CNNs. Before trusting lr=1e-3 by default, the best MLP config was re-trained at three starting learning rates to check it wasn't an arbitrary choice.

lr=1e-2 (unstable, best test 34.7%) lr=1e-3 (chosen, best test 45.7%) lr=1e-4 (slower, best test 42.6%)

The sweep confirmed lr=1e-3 wasn't arbitrary: too high and the wide MLP plateaus around 39% validation accuracy with a noisy trajectory; too low and it converges more slowly without catching up in the epoch budget. The augmentation ablation on CNN-Res tells a similar story about deliberate choices paying off — dropping RandomCrop/Flip/ColorJitter/Rotation let training accuracy hit 99.5% while test accuracy fell to 64.1%, a 35.4pt train–test gap versus 12.7pt with augmentation on. Across all three approaches the single hardest confusion is the same: lakeside and seashore, both waterside landscape shots, are confused in both directions at every stage (40/100 hand-crafted, up to 29/100 MLP, 26/100 CNN-Res) — architecture narrows this gap but doesn't close it. The comparison is airtight within this notebook, since the baseline and both deep-learning approaches share the same class draw, split and test images, but the 55.9% baseline figure differs from Part 1's reported 52.9% because a fresh 15-class sample was drawn for this coursework rather than reusing the exact same classes. A single train/val/test split with no cross-validation is also the trade-off made to afford training six deep models plus a learning-rate sweep and an ablation.

Data

5,250 training images, augmented on the fly

15 classes are freshly sampled from TinyImageNet100 and split alphabetically per class: the first 350 images for training, the next 50 for validation, the last 100 for test — 5,250 / 750 / 1,500 images, perfectly balanced across classes. Training images only are augmented (random horizontal flip, random crop with 8px padding, colour jitter, rotation up to 15°) and every image is normalised with per-channel statistics computed from the training set (mean ≈ 0.49/0.46/0.42, std ≈ 0.28/0.28/0.30).
