No deep learning allowed: 52.9% across 15 classes from SIFT alone

Local feature detectors were built for 500×500 photographs, not 64×64 crops. Pushing SIFT's parameters hard enough to compensate — then encoding what it finds with Fisher Vectors instead of Bag-of-Words — turned a barely-better-than-chance pipeline into a linear SVM that gets almost eight times better than random guessing, with clear, explicable failure modes left over.

01 · Parameter tuning outworked everything else

Loosening SIFT's keypoint gates — more octave layers, a near-zero contrast threshold, less smoothing — took mean detected features per image from 36 to 920. Holding Fisher Vector encoding fixed, that alone lifted accuracy from 35.1% to 52.9%.

02 · Encoding mattered almost as much as features

Swapping Bag-of-Words for Fisher Vectors added 9–13pt at every feature-extractor setting tried: +10.6pt for baseline SIFT, +12.3pt for the tuned version.

03 · Errors cluster on background, not object

The three worst confusions — alp→seashore, acorn→bell pepper, plate→cauliflower — each account for 18 of 100 test images. The model's worst class, plunger, is right only 18% of the time.

Methods

Six experiments, one clear winner: SIFT tuned hard, encoded with Fisher Vectors

SIFT was the obvious starting point over ORB: at default settings, ORB finds no keypoints at all in 4,835 of 6,000 training images, while SIFT finds at least one in all but 8. But SIFT itself was designed for 500×500 photographs, not 64×64 crops — at default parameters it surfaces a mean of just 36 features per image. Loosening every parameter that gates keypoint acceptance (more octave layers, a contrast threshold cut from 0.04 to 0.001, a wider edge threshold, less smoothing) pushed that to 920, at the cost of admitting real noise. Fisher Vectors, which encode first- and second-order deviations from a 128-component GMM rather than forcing a hard visual-word assignment, were chosen over Bag-of-Words to average that noise back out; a linear SVM with C grid-searched over four values via 3-fold CV closed the loop.

Grid search picked C=1e-5 by 3-fold cross-validated accuracy for every configuration; the confusion matrix (1,500 test images, 100 per class) shows the errors aren't random — alp/seashore, acorn/bell-pepper/cauliflower, and plate/cauliflower are the three heaviest confusions, at 18 of 100 each, consistent with the model keying on shared background texture (foliage, sea-blue, dirt) rather than object shape. The obvious limitation: 52.9% is close to the historical ceiling for hand-crafted features on data like this, and the "optimisation" that got there is fundamentally a workaround — trading detection precision for raw feature volume and letting Fisher Vectors average out the resulting noise, not a genuinely more discriminative descriptor. The result also rests on one fixed 15-class draw and one random seed; a different sample of classes could easily reorder the weaker configurations.

Data

64×64 crops, built for detectors designed at 500×500

15 classes are sampled with a fixed seed from TinyImageNet100's full class list, then sorted alphabetically for reproducibility: 400 training and 100 test images each, 6,000 and 1,500 images total — a clean 80/20 split with no class imbalance to control for. The complication is resolution: every image is a 64×64 downsampled crop, a twelfth of the linear resolution these detectors were designed against, and it shows directly in how few local features either one can find.
