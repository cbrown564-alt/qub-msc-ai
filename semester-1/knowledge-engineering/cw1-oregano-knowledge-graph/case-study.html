823,005 biomedical facts, formalised into seventeen predicates — only two hold universally

The OREGANO biomedical knowledge graph packs 823,005 compound–protein–gene–disease–pathway triples into eleven entity types. Parsing its ontology into first‑order logic and testing every predicate as a universally-quantified statement shows the graph is exhaustively documented in exactly two places — everywhere else, coverage is partial by construction.

01 · Skewed by design

The largest relation, decrease_efficacy, is 215,222 links — over a quarter of the graph on its own. That's expected: OREGANO is assembled from drug-interaction and side-effect databases, not sampled to be balanced.

02 · One relation resists a single rule

has_target is the only predicate that needs a disjunction — (Compound∧Molecule) ∨ (Compound∧Protein) — because OREGANO uses one relation name for two genuinely different target types.

03 · Universal claims mostly fail

Of 31,652 compounds, only 3,503 (11%) have a recorded has_activity link. Only has_phenotype (Disease) and gene_product_of (Protein) hold as ∀ statements across their entire subject class.

Methods

Ontology first, first-order logic second, then check the ∀ claims empirically

Domain and range for each predicate were read directly off the graph — grouping every unique (subject_class, relation, object_class) triple gave a snapshot of the ontology's structure, which was rebuilt in owlready2 as a populated OWL graph so relations, instances and attributes could be queried directly rather than re-derived from the raw TSV each time. Each of the 17 core predicates (excluding the literal-valued has_code) was then written as ∀x,∀y(predicate(x,y)) ⇒ domain(x) ∧ range(y), and tested for whether the stronger ∀x∈domain (∃y∈range) reading actually holds, by computing what fraction of each subject class the relation actually covers.

The one deliberately-broken symmetry is has_target, which OREGANO's authors overload across Molecule and Protein ranges — handled here with an explicit disjunction rather than forcing a single range. Beyond that, the coverage check is a statistical proof-by-counterexample, not an axiomatic guarantee: it confirms a ∀ claim only insofar as the snapshot graph itself is complete, and OREGANO is known to be a partial extraction from source databases like DrugBank. A single populated-instance spot-check (querying PROTEIN:1's gene_product_of value against the raw TSV) was used to confirm the OWL population step preserved the original triples correctly, but this is a sanity check on construction, not a validation of the biology itself.

Data

Eighteen relation types, one of them a literal, and a very long tail

OREGANO v2.1 ships as 823,005 subject–relation–object triples.
