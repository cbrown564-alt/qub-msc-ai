01 · Feature engineering cuts both ways

The 57 engineered volume/volatility/trend/momentum features improved every tree-based model but made Ridge worse — the curse of dimensionality in miniature, and the reason model and feature selection were run as separate experiments.

02 · Ten features beat fifty-seven

Selecting the top 10 features by importance — led by OBV, the lower Bollinger band and ADX — trained roughly 2.9× faster than the full set and matched or beat its directional accuracy on 4 of 6 tickers.

03 · Efficiency picked the winner

LightGBM and XGBoost reached near-identical validation scores after tuning, but LightGBM's 100-iteration hyperparameter search ran in 34s against XGBoost's 1152s — decisive once daily refitting was the plan.

Methods

Five models, two feature sets, one leakage-safe harness

Ridge established a baseline on raw OHLCV data; ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost and LightGBM were then compared against it and each other, first with default hyperparameters and then after an extensive 100-iteration RandomizedSearchCV per model. Sequence models (LSTM, Transformers) were ruled out up front on time and complexity grounds. All training and evaluation ran through a custom expanding-window, leakage-safe harness (mltester.py) built specifically to guard against the lookahead bias and overfitting that plague financial time-series ML.

Feature selection trimmed 57 candidates to the 10 most important (by split-gain importance) after confirming that a 5–20 feature range performed best in a sweep — fewer features meant materially faster training with no consistent loss of accuracy, and a lower over-fitting risk. Hyperparameters were tuned once with an extensive randomised search, then fixed for fast retraining, which is what made the daily-refit results above practical to compute at all. The honest caveats: directional accuracy across all six tickers, even for the winning model, tops out in the low-to-mid 60s% — this is financial return prediction with a genuinely low signal:noise ratio; performance also degrades noticeably in higher-volatility periods such as the early 2020s; and feature engineering that helps LightGBM can actively hurt a linear model, so none of these conclusions transfer automatically to a different model family.

Data

Six sector ETFs, fifteen years, fifty-seven candidate features

22,638 daily OHLCV rows across six industry-sector ETFs — XLE (Energy), XLF (Financials), XLI (Industrials), XLK (Technology), XLP (Consumer Staples), XLV (Healthcare) — from January 2010 to December 2024, roughly 3,773 trading days per ticker. From raw prices, 57 candidate features were engineered across volume (OBV, AD, rolling volume), volatility (NATR, Bollinger Bands), trend (smoothed moving averages) and momentum (MACD signal, ADX) categories, then ranked by importance.
