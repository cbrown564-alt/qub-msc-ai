An unsupervised model splits 26 years of markets into three moods — and tells a slow crash from a sudden one

A Gaussian Mixture Model, given only rolling volatility and a market-synchronisation signal for six sector ETFs since 1999, clustered the market into three regimes without ever seeing a labelled crisis date. It separates the fast, violent panics of 2008 and 2020 from the slower, lower-intensity 2000 dot-com correction — and the regimes it finds turn out to have Sharpe ratios a full point apart.

01 · The panic label is precise

Panic days map exclusively onto the 2008 financial crisis and 2020 covid crash (115 of 6,748 days, 1.7%). The 2000 dot-com crash — lower-intensity, longer-lived — lands in Correction instead, exactly as its volatility profile suggests it should.

02 · Statistics and judgment disagreed, twice

The metrics preferred dropping market synchronisation entirely (best BIC) and a slower 90-day volatility window (best silhouette). Financial intuition about how fast a panic needs recognising overruled both.

03 · The regimes barely help the forecast they were built for

Fed as one-hot features into the CW1 LightGBM pipeline, they lift directional accuracy by only +0.4pp (50.7% → 51.1%) — the model's existing volatility features already captured most of that signal.

Methods

GMM over K-Means, full covariance over diagonal — then overruled on its own metrics

GMM was chosen over K-Means because financial regimes have elliptical, heteroskedastic shapes that a hard-boundary, equal-variance clustering can't represent. A full-covariance GMM confirmed this directly, beating spherical and diagonal covariance on both BIC (55,860 vs. 90,608 / 74,260) and silhouette score (0.38 vs. 0.33 / 0.28) at k=3.

silhouette score vs. rolling-volatility window width · 30 days chosen for responsiveness over the 90-day statistical optimum

Neither BIC nor silhouette score actually endorses the final configuration. BIC kept improving all the way out to a 180-day window and to excluding market synchronisation altogether (its single best setting scored 39,129 vs. the chosen setup's 55,860), while silhouette peaked at a 90-day window (0.40) and even preferred k=2 outright (0.42) over k=3's 0.38. The 30-day window, the inclusion of market synchronisation, and k=3 were all chosen against the numbers, on financial-judgment grounds: a regime detector that takes 90 days to notice a shift is too slow for a crisis that unfolds in weeks, and 2 regimes can't distinguish a sector correction from a market-wide panic. That's a legitimate but subjective call, and it means the headline regime boundaries reflect domain reasoning as much as any single optimised metric — a point the notebook itself is explicit about.

Data

Mostly calm, rarely correcting, almost never panicking

6,778 daily closes for six sector ETFs — XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLV — from January 1999 to December 2025, reduced to two standardised features: 30-day rolling volatility and market synchronisation (the top eigenvalue of the 30-day rolling correlation matrix). After the rolling windows are applied, 6,748 usable trading days remain for clustering.
